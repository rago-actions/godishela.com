What a Forward Deployed Engineer Actually Does

Most engineers ship a feature and move on to the next ticket. A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) stays with the problem until the customer's system actually works.

Where the role came from

Palantir popularised the model. Instead of handing customers a product and a manual, it sent engineers to sit with them, build against their real data, and turn one-off fixes into features the product could keep.

AI has made the role common again. Connecting a model to an enterprise's data, permissions and workflows is bespoke work, and an API on its own doesn't do it. Model providers and enterprise software companies are building FDE teams to close the gap between "it works in the demo" and "it works in production".

What the job is

An FDE is a senior software engineer embedded with a customer or internal team. They own the outcome end to end, not a slice of it. The job sits where three things meet:

  • Engineering: writing and running production code.
  • Solution design: deciding what to build, and what not to.
  • Communication: explaining trade-offs to people who don't read code.

The measure is whether the customer got the result, not whether the tickets closed.

Five skills that matter

  1. Coding under ambiguity. Goals change mid-week and the codebase is ten years old. You still ship something reliable.
  2. Translating business into systems. "We need to cut reporting time in half" has to become a design someone can build.
  3. Owning delivery past the ticket. Done means it runs in production and keeps running.
  4. Building tools that multiply people. Scripts, bots and small platforms that save a team hours every week are often worth more than the feature itself.
  5. Working across teams. Most blockers live between teams, not inside one. Getting engineering, product and leadership moving together is part of the job.

The same skills, pointed inward

The classic FDE works with one external customer. The same habits work just as well inside a large platform company, where the "customer" is every team that depends on shared infrastructure. Owning outcomes, building tools that remove toil, and coordinating across teams improve the platform for everyone on it.

You don't need the title to work this way.
