The 12-Month Buffer: Kakeibo for Layoff Season

Tech layoffs keep coming. A paper-and-pen Japanese budgeting method and simple arithmetic can get you to twelve months of runway.

What's happening

Layoffs.fyi lists about 135,000 tech job cuts across 331 companies in 2026, as of early November. That figure is soft: it includes headcount drops at companies like Oracle and Dell that are not all layoffs. Even so, the direction is clear. Salesforce cut roughly 1,000 roles in February across teams including marketing, product management, analytics, Agentforce and Heroku, and in November the press reported another round.

This is not a post about any one company. It is about what the news tells every one of us: a paycheck is not a guarantee, and the only protection that works before the email arrives is cash.

Why twelve months, not three

The usual advice is three to six months of expenses. I would aim higher if you work in tech, for three reasons:

  • Hiring is slow when many people search at once. Layoffs push thousands of similar candidates into the same openings.
  • Severance is not runway. It is a bonus on top of runway, and it varies by country, tenure and company. Do not plan on it.
  • Some costs spike on day one. Health insurance moves from employer-subsidized to full price. If you are on a work visa abroad, such as an H-1B in the US, the grace period after losing a job is 60 days, so a search can force a big decision.

Step 1: Compute your number

Your target is not twelve months of your salary. It is twelve months of what you must pay to stay afloat.

Buffer = (Survival expenses + full-price health insurance) × 12

Example: say you take home ₹3,00,000 a month. Rent, groceries, utilities, phone and EMIs come to ₹1,05,000. Add ₹15,000 for health insurance at full price, and your Survival number is ₹1,20,000 a month. That makes a buffer of ₹14,40,000. Everything else, about ₹1,80,000, is Optional, Culture, Extra and savings. Use your own figures.

Step 2: Use Kakeibo to find the real numbers

Kakeibo means “household financial diary.” Hani Motoko, Japan's first female journalist, introduced it in 1904. It is a notebook, a pen and four questions each month:

  1. How much money comes in this month?
  2. How much do I want to save?
  3. How much am I actually spending?
  4. How can I do better?

Write every expense by hand at the end of the day, in one of four columns: Survival, Optional, Culture and Extra. The hand-writing is the point. Typing into an app is too frictionless to change behavior.

The Survival column gives you the number for Step 1. Most people discover it is smaller than they feared, and the Optional column is larger.

Step 3: Pay the buffer first

The core idea of Kakeibo is simple: do not save what is left after spending; spend what is left after saving. On payday, pay fixed costs, then move your savings amount into a separate account before anything else. Automate it so it does not depend on willpower.

Step 4: Do the timeline math

Months to goal = Buffer ÷ Monthly savings

Saving 20% of ₹3,00,000 is ₹60,000 a month, so ₹14,40,000 takes 24 months. At 30%, or ₹90,000 a month, it takes 16. It is a long road, so break it into tiers and celebrate each one:

  • 1 month (₹1.2 lakh): you can survive a surprise bill or a short gap.
  • 3 months (₹3.6 lakh): a standard emergency fund.
  • 6 months (₹7.2 lakh): you can say no to a bad offer.
  • 12 months (₹14.4 lakh): you can search carefully, retrain, or take a pay cut for a better role.

Step 5: Cutting spend works twice

Trimming ₹20,000 of Optional spending a month does two things. It lifts savings from ₹60,000 to ₹80,000, which cuts the timeline from 24 months to 18. And if the cut becomes a habit, your Survival number can drop too, which shrinks the buffer itself. A smaller lifestyle makes the target easier to hit and easier to survive on.

Where to keep it

Boring and liquid: a high-yield savings account, a money market fund or short-term government bills. This money is insurance, not an investment. It must be there in a downturn, so keep it out of stocks and crypto.

The rules

  • Touch it only for loss of income or a true emergency, not a vacation or a sale.
  • If you spend it, rebuild it before you resume optional spending.
  • Review it at the end of each month, the way Kakeibo does, and adjust.

These are my personal views, not financial advice and not the view of my employer. Layoff figures come from public reports and trackers and may change.
