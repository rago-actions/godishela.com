Noisy Neighbors in Shared Infrastructure

When one tenant hogs shared CPU, memory, disk or network, everyone else on the same hardware slows down. Here's why it happens and how to contain it.

Why it happens

  • Resource contention: one tenant's high demand drains resources everyone shares.
  • Misconfigured applications: poorly tuned apps take far more than they need.
  • Weak isolation: tenants in a multi-tenant setup aren't separated well enough.
  • Dynamic workloads: sudden spikes overwhelm shared capacity.

What it does to everyone else

  • Slower responses for other tenants.
  • Unpredictable behavior: problems come and go, which makes them hard to troubleshoot.
  • Outages in the worst cases.

How to contain it

Limits and throttling

  • Enforce resource quotas to cap usage.
  • Add rate limits to stop overuse.

Isolation

  • Isolate workloads with containers or virtual machines.
  • Give critical workloads dedicated hardware.

Monitoring and alerts

  • Watch usage in real time with tools like Grafana or Datadog.
  • Alert early so you can act before other tenants feel it.

Scaling

  • Auto-scale during workload spikes.
  • Use predictive scaling based on usage trends.

Scheduling and placement

  • Use smarter scheduling to spread workloads evenly.
  • Avoid overcommitting resources.

Dedicated pools

  • Set aside resources for specific tenants or workloads that can't share.

No single fix solves it. Containing noisy neighbors takes resource management, good monitoring and deliberate design together, so shared infrastructure stays fair and efficient.
